      <h1 id="diseaseTitle">Understanding cardiometabolic diseases</h1>

      <p>Cardiometabolic diseases are a group of conditions involving the heart and blood vessels, blood sugar regulation, and the body's use of fats and energy. They often share risk factors and can occur together.</p>

      <p class="muted">This page provides general health education. It does not diagnose disease or replace advice from a qualified healthcare professional.</p>

      <div class="nav-links">

        <a href="#types">Explore disease types</a>

    <section class="section" id="types">

      <h2>Types of cardiometabolic diseases</h2>

      <p class="muted">These conditions are related, but they are not identical. A person may have one or several at the same time.</p>

      <div class="grid">

        <article class="card">

          <span class="pill">Blood sugar</span>

          <h3>1. Type 2 diabetes</h3>

          <p>A long-term condition in which the body does not use insulin effectively and blood glucose becomes too high.</p>

          <p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> Over time, it can damage blood vessels, nerves, kidneys, eyes, and the heart.</p>

        </article>

        <article class="card">

          <span class="pill">Blood pressure</span>

          <h3>2. Hypertension (high blood pressure)</h3>

          <p>Blood pressure that remains higher than recommended. It often has no noticeable symptoms, so measurement is important.</p>

          <p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> It increases the risk of heart disease, stroke, kidney disease, and other complications.</p>

        </article>

        <article class="card">

          <span class="pill">Blood fats</span>

          <h3>3. Dyslipidaemia</h3>

          <p>Unhealthy levels of blood lipids, such as high LDL cholesterol or triglycerides, or low HDL cholesterol.</p>

          <p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> Some lipid patterns can contribute to plaque buildup in arteries.</p>

        </article>

        <article class="card">

          <span class="pill">Heart and blood vessels</span>

          <h3>4. Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease</h3>

          <p>Conditions caused by plaque buildup in arteries, including coronary artery disease and some types of stroke and peripheral artery disease.</p>

          <p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> Reduced blood flow can lead to serious events such as a heart attack or stroke.</p>

        </article>

        <article class="card">

          <span class="pill">Metabolic pattern</span>

          <h3>5. Metabolic syndrome</h3>

          <p>A cluster of risk factors that may include increased waist circumference, elevated blood pressure, high blood glucose, high triglycerides, and low HDL cholesterol.</p>

          <p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> Having several of these factors together raises the likelihood of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.</p>

        </article>

        <article class="card">

          <span class="pill">Liver health</span>

          <h3>6. Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD)</h3>

          <p>A condition involving excess fat in the liver associated with metabolic risk factors. It was previously commonly called non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD).</p>

          <p><strong>Why it matters:</strong> In some people it can progress to liver inflammation, scarring, or more advanced liver disease.</p>

        </article>

      </div>

    </section>


    <section class="section" id="risk-factors">

      <h2>Common risk factors</h2>

      <p class="muted">Risk factors increase the chance of developing a condition; they do not guarantee that someone will develop it. Some can be changed, while others cannot.</p>

      <div class="risk-grid">

        <div class="risk-item"><strong>Low physical activity</strong>Limited movement can affect blood pressure, insulin sensitivity, weight, and cholesterol.</div>

        <div class="risk-item"><strong>Unbalanced eating pattern</strong>Frequent intake of highly processed foods, excess salt, added sugars, and unhealthy fats can increase risk.</div>

        <div class="risk-item"><strong>Tobacco use</strong>Smoking and other tobacco exposure damage blood vessels and increase cardiovascular risk.</div>

        <div class="risk-item"><strong>Excess body fat, especially abdominal fat</strong>Can be associated with insulin resistance, high blood pressure, and abnormal blood lipids.</div>

        <div class="risk-item"><strong>Insufficient or irregular sleep</strong>Can be associated with changes in appetite, glucose regulation, and blood pressure.</div>

        <div class="risk-item"><strong>High blood pressure</strong>Often has no symptoms but places extra strain on blood vessels and the heart.</div>

        <div class="risk-item"><strong>Abnormal blood glucose</strong>Prediabetes and diabetes are important indicators of metabolic risk.</div>

        <div class="risk-item"><strong>Unhealthy blood lipid levels</strong>High LDL cholesterol or triglycerides, and low HDL cholesterol, can contribute to risk.</div>

        <div class="risk-item"><strong>Family history and genetics</strong>A close relative with diabetes or early cardiovascular disease can increase risk.</div>
